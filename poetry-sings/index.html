缘起Our Story

诗渡山海从中国诗词与艺术歌曲出发，探寻诗意在不同时代的音乐回响。首场演出汇集早期经典与当代作品，展现诗歌与音乐交融的丰富面貌。传统诗词的意境与情韵，在旋律中绵延，也在作曲家的笔下生发新的表达。我们希望借这些作品，让更多听众领略中文的声韵之美，感受字句间的深情与中国艺术歌曲的文化底蕴。

由此启程，诗渡山海期待走近更多文化与音乐传统，分享其中的诗意与故事。愿来自不同土地的诗句与旋律，带着各自的记忆与情感越过山海，让我们在聆听中发现共鸣，在歌声中走近彼此。

Where Poetry Sings Across the Seas begins with Chinese poetry and art song, exploring how verse finds new resonance in music across generations. Its inaugural recital brings together early classics and contemporary works, revealing the rich interplay of poetry and music. The imagery and feeling of traditional verse live on in melody, finding fresh expression through each composer’s imagination. Through these works, we invite listeners to discover the beauty of the Chinese language, the emotions held within its words, and the rich cultural heritage of Chinese art song.

From this beginning, Where Poetry Sings Across the Seas looks forward to exploring more cultures and musical traditions, sharing their poetry and stories. May poems and melodies from different lands cross mountains and seas with the memories and emotions they carry, finding echoes in our own lives and drawing us closer through song.

01

踏雪寻梅Winter Plum Blossom

黄自作曲 · 刘雪庵词

Music by Huang Zi · Text by Liu Xue’an

导赏Notes

黄自是中国近现代重要作曲家与音乐教育家，尤以艺术歌曲创作著称；刘雪庵则是中国近现代作曲家、音乐教育家及词作者。

《踏雪寻梅》是黄自最广为流传的艺术歌曲之一，描绘了雪后初晴、踏雪寻梅的冬日情景。刘雪庵的歌词勾勒出一幅生动而富有诗意的画面：冬雪初霁，梅香浮动，行人骑驴过桥，清脆的铃声一路相伴。

黄自以清新明快的音乐语言赋予这一画面鲜活的生命力。轻盈跳跃的旋律，以及反复出现的“叮当”声，既让人联想到驴铃清脆的声响，也营造出旅途中欢快而富有节奏感的步伐。钢琴声部则进一步加强了音乐的流动感与灵动气息。

在轻松明朗的表象之下，这首作品也蕴含着独特的中国传统审美。梅花凌寒而放，在中国文化中常被赋予坚韧、高洁与清雅的象征意义。因此，《踏雪寻梅》不仅描写了冬日自然之美，也展现了中国文人亲近自然、寄情诗乐的生活情趣与精神追求。

Huang Zi was an important Chinese composer and music educator of the early twentieth century, particularly known for his art songs. Liu Xue’an was a Chinese composer, educator, and lyricist whose work also played an important role in the development of modern Chinese song.

This is one of Huang Zi’s best-known art songs. It depicts a bright winter scene after a snowfall, as a traveler sets out in search of blooming plum blossoms. Liu Xue’an’s text paints a vivid and poetic picture: the snow is clear, the fragrance of plum blossoms fills the air, and a traveler crosses a bridge on a donkey while the crispy sound of bells accompanies the journey.

Huang brings this scene to life through fresh and buoyant musical writing. The light, playful melody and repeated “ding-dang” figures suggest the ringing of the donkeys bells while also creating a lively sense of forward motion. The piano further enhances the music’s fluidity and animated character.

Beneath its cheerful surface, the song also reflects a distinctly Chinese poetic sensibility. Because plum blossoms bloom in the depth of winter, they have long symbolized resilience, purity, and quiet elegance in Chinese culture. The song therefore celebrates not only the beauty of a winter landscape, but also the traditional Chinese ideal of finding artistic and spiritual pleasure in nature, poetry, and music.

02

红豆词Red Love Beans

刘雪庵作曲 · 曹雪芹词

Music by Liu Xue’an · Text by Cao Xueqin

导赏Notes

同样是刘雪庵作曲；歌词作者曹雪芹则是中国古典文学名著《红楼梦》的作者。

《红豆词》的歌词选自中国四大名著之一《红楼梦》第二十八回，是贾宝玉所唱的一支曲子。“红豆”在中国古典诗词中历来象征相思与眷恋，而开篇“滴不尽相思血泪抛红豆”便将这种难以排遣的思念推向极致。此后，春花、秋月、孤枕、更漏等一系列意象层层展开，描绘出因思念而辗转难眠、愁绪难解的内心世界。

刘雪庵的音乐含蓄而婉转，以富有中国传统韵味的旋律语言承载古典诗词的节奏与声韵。钢琴声部则以流动的织体和细腻的和声色彩烘托歌声，使情感在克制之中逐渐加深。作品并不以强烈的戏剧冲突表现爱情，而是在反复咏叹与绵延的旋律中，将思念转化为一种悠远而无法消散的情绪。

从“相思血泪”到结尾“青山隐隐，绿水悠悠”，《红豆词》将个人的情感融入自然景象之中。那份无尽的眷恋仿佛随着山水不断延伸，也使这首作品兼具《红楼梦》的文学意境与中国艺术歌曲特有的含蓄之美。

This song is also composed by Liu Xue’an. The text is by Cao Xueqin, author of Dream of the Red Chamber, one of the great classics of Chinese literature. The text of Song of the Red Bean comes from Chapter 28 of Dream of the Red Chamber, where it is sung by the character Jia Baoyu. In classical Chinese poetry, the red bean has long symbolized longing and romantic attachment. The opening line immediately evokes an overwhelming sense of yearning. Images of spring flowers, the autumn moon, a solitary pillow, and the passing hours of the night gradually unfold, portraying a restless inner world shaped by sleeplessness, longing, and sorrow.

Liu Xue’an’s music is restrained and lyrical, rather than portraying love through dramatic conflict, the song transforms longing into something lingering and almost timeless through repeated lyrical gestures and sustained melodic lines.

From the opening image of “tears of blood” to the final vision of “the distant blue mountain, and endlessly flowing green water” Song of the Red Bean merges personal emotion with the natural landscape. The sense of yearning seems to extend beyond the individual and into the mountains and rivers themselves, bringing together the literary atmosphere of Dream of the Red Chamber and the understated expressive beauty characteristic of Chinese art song.

03

思乡Homesickness

黄自作曲 · 韦瀚章词

Music by Huang Zi · Text by Wei Hanzhang

导赏Notes

黄自作曲，韦瀚章则是中国早期重要的歌词作家之一。《思乡》创作于 1932年，是二人最具代表性的合作作品之一。

歌词从清明过后的暮春景色展开：“柳丝系绿”，春意正浓，游子却独自凭栏，在明媚的景色中愈发感受到孤独。墙外传来的杜鹃啼声仿佛一遍遍诉说着“不如归去”，由此唤起层层叠叠的乡愁。落花随水而去，主人公于是向花发问：流水是否向南流去？若是如此，“我愿与他同去”。自然景物在这里不再只是风景，而成为思乡之情的寄托。

黄自以细腻而富有诗意的音乐语言将这种情感缓缓展开。声乐旋律随着歌词自然起伏，从最初的安静凝望逐渐转向愈发深切的思念。黄自曾特别向韦瀚章解释，钢琴部分中融入了潺潺流水与杜鹃啼鸣的音乐形象，使诗中的景物仿佛在声音中真实浮现。

《思乡》的动人之处正在于这种含蓄：作品没有直接宣泄悲痛，而是借柳丝、杜鹃、落花与流水表达无法言说的思念。最终，那份“归去”的愿望仿佛随着流水向远方延伸，使个人的乡愁融入中国古典诗词中悠远而深沉的“思归”意境。

With music by Huang Zi and poetry by Wei Hanzhang, an important lyricist of early twentieth-century China, Homesickness was composed in 1932 and remains one of their most enduring collaborations.

The poem opens in late spring, just after the Qingming Festival (Tomb-Sweep Day). Willow branches have turned green and the season is alive with beauty, yet the traveler stands alone by the railing, made all the more aware of his solitude by the brightness around him. Beyond the wall, the call of the cuckoo seems to repeat the ancient refrain, “Better to return home,” awakening an ever-deepening sense of longing. Fallen blossoms drift away on the stream, and the speaker wonders whether the water is flowing southward. If so, he says, “I would follow it home.” The landscape thus becomes more than scenery: it becomes a reflection of the traveler’s inner world.

Huang Zi unfolds this longing through music of remarkable delicacy and poetic restraint. The vocal line follows the natural contours of the text, moving from quiet contemplation toward increasingly intense yearning. Huang once explained to Wei Hanzhang that the piano writing evokes both the murmur of flowing water and the distant call of the cuckoo, allowing the natural images of the poem to emerge almost tangibly through sound.

The expressive power of Homesickness lies in what it leaves unspoken. Rather than voicing grief directly, the song allows willow branches, cuckoo calls, falling blossoms, and flowing water to carry the weight of memory and desire. By the end, the longing to return home seems to drift into the distance with the stream itself, transforming personal homesickness into the timeless Chinese poetic image of yearning for home.

青主作曲 · 李之仪词

Music by Qing Zhu · Text by Li Zhiyi

导赏Notes

青主，原名廖尚果，是中国近现代重要的作曲家与音乐理论家，其艺术歌曲创作融合了西方音乐语言与中国古典诗词的审美意境。歌词选自北宋词人李之仪的《卜算子·我住长江头》，以质朴而深情的语言表达了相隔千里的恋人之间绵延不绝的思念。

诗歌以长江为中心意象，从“我住长江头，君住长江尾”展开，将两位恋人置于同一条江水的两端。江水既象征着无法逾越的距离，也成为维系彼此情感的纽带。“日日思君不见君，共饮长江水”，将相思的苦涩与心灵的相依巧妙交织。随着“此水几时休？此恨何时已？”的反复追问，思念仿佛化作永不停息的江流，在时间与空间中无尽延伸。

这首作品将离别的忧伤与爱情的忠贞融为一体。长江的流水既诉说着相隔千里的无奈，也象征着永不止息的眷恋。最终，“只愿君心似我心，定不负相思意”将无尽的思念凝结为一句温柔而坚定的承诺：纵然山水相隔，只要两心相知，爱情便能如江水一般绵延不绝。

Qing Zhu, born Liao Shangguo, was an important Chinese composer and music theorist of the early twentieth century. His art songs bring together Western musical language and the poetic sensibilities of classical Chinese literature. The text is drawn from Bu Suan Zi by Li Zhiyi, a poet of the Northern Song Dynasty, whose simple yet deeply expressive verses convey the enduring longing between two lovers separated by a great distance.

The poem unfolds around the Yangtze River, opening with the lines “I dwell near the Yangtze River’s source, While you farthest down its course.” The lovers stand at opposite ends of the same river, whose waters become both a symbol of their separation and an invisible thread connecting their hearts. “I miss you day after day, but you’re far away. From the same river we drink, relieving my pain.” These words intertwine the sorrow of separation with the intimacy of a shared existence. As the poet repeatedly asks, “When will the river no more flow? When will my sorrow no longer grow?” longing seems to merge with the ceaseless current, stretching endlessly across time and distance.

The End of Yangtze River lies in its delicate balance between the sorrow of separation and the steadfastness of love. The flowing Yangtze embodies both the pain of distance and the enduring bond between two hearts. In the final words, “Be your heart as steadfast as mine, Then not in vain for you I pine,” the endless longing finds its resolution in a tender yet resolute promise: though mountains and rivers may keep two lovers apart, a love sustained by mutual devotion can flow as endlessly as the river itself.

关雎Crying Ospreys

赵季平作曲 · 选自《诗经·周南》

Music by Zhao Jiping · Text from the Book of Songs (Zhou Nan)

导赏Notes

在中国最古老的诗歌总集中，爱情以一声鸟鸣开始。《关雎》位居《诗经》三百余篇之首，以“关关雎鸠，在河之洲”勾勒出一幅宁静而生动的自然画卷。河洲之上，水鸟相互应和；流水之间，荇菜随波轻摇。就在这片温柔的景色中，一段关于爱慕、思念与追寻的故事悄然展开。两千多年前的诗句没有华丽的爱情宣言，却以最质朴的语言，道出了人类亘古不变的情感。

最初的倾慕是如此美好，然而爱情并不总能如愿。白日的思念延续至漫漫长夜，辗转反侧之间，渴望与惆怅交织。诗中反复出现的荇菜，伴随着主人公不断回旋的心绪，将难以言说的相思融入水波的流动之中。直到现实中尚未实现的爱情，在想象里化作琴瑟相和、钟鼓齐鸣的美好场景。那些曾经无处安放的思念，终于在音乐中找到了寄托。古老的文字仿佛跨越时光，让两千多年前的一颗心，在今日依然能够被听见。

孔子曾以 “乐而不淫，哀而不伤”赞美《关雎》，道出了中国古典诗歌中情感与节制并存的审美理想。这里的爱情既有热烈的渴望，也有温柔的克制；既经历求而不得的苦楚，也始终怀抱对美好的信念。诗中的琴瑟与钟鼓或许只响起于想象之中，但那份渴望被爱、被理解、被回应的心情，却从未因岁月流逝而改变。

In China’s oldest surviving collection of poetry, love begins with the call of a bird. Crying Ospreys, the opening poem of The Book of Songs (Shijing), evokes a scene of quiet beauty with the lines Waterbirds call to one another along the river, while delicate water plants sway gently with the current. Against this tranquil landscape, a story of affection, longing, and the pursuit of love quietly unfolds. Written more than two thousand years ago, these verses offer no grand declarations of passion. Instead, through the simplest of words, they give voice to emotions that have remained timeless.

The tenderness of first attraction is beautiful, yet love does not always find its answer. Thoughts of the beloved linger from daylight into the long hours of night, where desire and melancholy intertwine amid restless, sleepless longing. The recurring images of water plants drifting with the current seem to echo the speaker’s unsettled heart, carrying his unspoken yearning into the gentle movement of the river. At last, the love that remains unfulfilled in reality finds expression in imagination, where lutes and harps sound in harmony, and bells and drums resound in joyful celebration. Longing, once without an answer, finds its refuge in music. Across the centuries, these ancient words seem to come alive, allowing a heart that beat more than two thousand years ago to be heard once more.

Confucius once praised Crying Ospreys as “joyful without excess, sorrowful without despair,” capturing the classical Chinese ideal of harmony between emotion and restraint. The love portrayed here is both passionate and tender, marked by the sorrow of unfulfilled desire yet sustained by an unwavering hope for happiness. The lutes, harps, bells, and drums may sound only in the lover’s imagination, but the longing to be loved, to be understood, and to have one’s affection returned remains as deeply human today as it was centuries ago.

幽兰操Ode to the Orchid

赵季平作曲 · 韩愈词

Music by Zhao Jiping · Text by Han Yu

导赏Notes

一株生长在幽谷中的兰花，即使无人欣赏，依然静静散发芬芳。这一看似简单的自然景象，在中国传统文化中却承载着深刻的精神寓意。兰花被誉为“花中君子”，它的清雅不依赖外界的赞美，它的高洁也不因境遇而改变。

《幽兰操》正是借这一意象，探寻品格、理想与人生际遇之间的关系。

“不采而佩，于兰何伤？”诗人的发问并非感伤于无人赏识，而是在肯定一种不为外物所动的精神独立。随后出现的“文王梦熊，渭水泱泱”，则引入周文王寻访姜太公的典故，让诗歌从独处幽谷的兰花转向贤才与知音的相遇。兰花既可以独自芬芳，也期待有朝一日被真正理解。两种看似矛盾的愿望，共同构成了中国传统文人对于理想人格的想象。

赵季平的音乐让这种内在张力获得了声音。带有古典吟诵意味的旋律缓缓展开，仿佛诗人独自沉思；随着音乐的延伸，歌声逐渐展现出更加坚定的力量。这里没有喧嚣的赞颂，也不需要刻意渲染孤独。兰花的芬芳、君子的操守，以及对知音的期盼，都存在于一种安静而自持的气度之中。

An orchid blooms quietly in a secluded valley, releasing its fragrance even when no one is there to admire it. In Chinese culture, this seemingly simple image carries profound spiritual significance. Revered as one of the “Four Gentlemen” of Chinese art and literature, the orchid embodies integrity, humility, and quiet dignity. Its beauty does not depend upon admiration, nor does its virtue diminish in solitude. Ode to the Orchid draws upon this imagery to explore the relationship between personal integrity, ideals, and the uncertainties of human destiny.

“Even if her beauty no one appreciates, Charming and graceful as ever she remains.” The poet’s reflection is not merely a lament for unrecognized beauty, but an affirmation of inner independence: a spirit that remains unchanged by worldly judgment. The poem then turns to the historical allusion of King Wen of Zhou and his encounter with the wise Jiang Shang by the Wei River, shifting from the solitude of the orchid to the possibility of recognition and understanding. The orchid may flourish in solitude, yet it also awaits the moment when its worth is truly recognized. These seemingly opposing desires reflect a central ideal of the Chinese literary tradition: to remain faithful to one’s principles while longing for a kindred spirit who can understand them.

Zhao Jiping gives voice to this inward tension through music. The melody unfolds with the measured grace of classical poetic recitation, as though the poet were lost in solitary contemplation. As the music develops, the vocal line gradually acquires greater strength and conviction. There is no need for grand declarations or an exaggerated sense of loneliness. The orchid’s fragrance, the integrity of the noble spirit, and the quiet hope of finding a kindred soul all emerge through an atmosphere of restraint, dignity, and inner resolve.

春花秋月何时了When Will the Turning of Seasons End?

王龙作曲 · 邓垚编配 · 李煜词

Music by Wang Long · Arranged by Deng Yao · Text by Li Yu

导赏Notes

春花年年盛开，秋月岁岁依旧，而曾经拥有的一切，却再也无法重来。《春花秋月何时了》出自南唐后主李煜的词作《虞美人》。李煜原是一国之君，南唐灭亡后被俘至北宋都城汴京，从昔日的帝王沦为失去自由的阶下囚。曾经熟悉的宫阙、故土与往昔岁月，只能在记忆中寻觅。对于这位失去家国的词人而言，春花秋月不再是值得欣赏的良辰美景，反而成为不断提醒他往事已逝的残酷见证。

“小楼昨夜又东风，故国不堪回首月明中。”一个“又”字，道尽了时光无情的循环。东风依旧吹拂，明月依旧照耀，然而故国已不复存在。词人想象着昔日宫殿中精美的雕栏玉砌或许依然如故，但昔日的人事容颜早已改变。永恒的自然与无常的人世在此形成鲜明对照，而个人的哀愁也与一个王朝的兴衰交织在一起，使这首词超越了寻常的怀旧，成为对命运、失去与时间的深沉凝望。

全词最终凝结于一个流传千古的追问：“问君能有几多愁？恰似一江春水向东流。”愁绪不再是抽象的情感，而化作浩荡东去、永无止息的江水。王龙以逐渐增强的音乐张力回应这一意象，又在尾声中放缓音乐的步伐，让激烈的情绪渐渐归于平静。江水终将流向远方，时光亦无法倒转；而那份无法挽回的失落，却随着音乐与诗句，在时间的长河中久久回荡。

Spring blossoms return year after year, and the autumn moon shines as it always has, yet all that was once cherished can never be reclaimed. When Will the Turning of Seasons End? is drawn from Yu Meiren, a celebrated poem by Li Yu, the last ruler of the Southern Tang dynasty. Following the fall of his kingdom, Li Yu was taken captive to Bianjing, the capital of the Northern Song dynasty, where the former emperor lived as a prisoner, stripped of his throne and freedom. The palaces, landscapes, and familiar world of his past survived only in memory. For a man who had lost both his kingdom and his former life, the beauty of spring blossoms and the autumn moon no longer offered comfort, but became painful reminders of everything that could never return.

“Last night, the east wind blew past my small building again” The single word You (又, “again”) captures the merciless repetition of time. The spring breeze still returns, and the moon continues to shine, yet the kingdom to which they once belonged has vanished. Li Yu imagines that the intricately carved railings and jade steps of his former palace may still remain, while the faces and lives of those who once inhabited it have changed forever. The permanence of nature stands in stark contrast to the fragility of human existence. Personal grief becomes inseparable from the rise and fall of a dynasty, transforming the poem from a lament for the past into a profound meditation on fate, loss, and the passage of time.

The poem culminates in one of the most unforgettable images in Chinese literature: “If you ask me how many sorrows I have? They are as many as the waters of the river in spring, swollen with snowmelt, surging toward the east.” Grief is no longer an abstract emotion, but an immense, unceasing current flowing toward the distant horizon. Wang Long responds to this imagery with mounting musical intensity before gradually allowing the music to subside in the closing passage. The river flows onward, and time cannot be turned back. Yet the sorrow of all that has been lost continues to resonate, carried across the centuries by poetry and music.

钗头凤Phoenix Hairpin

周易作曲 · 陆游词

Music by Zhou Yi · Text by Lu You

导赏Notes

一场多年后的意外重逢，让一段早已失去的爱情重新浮现在眼前。《钗头凤》出自南宋诗人陆游之手，相传写于他与前妻唐婉在绍兴沈园的一次相遇。两人曾情深意笃，却因家族的反对而被迫分离。多年以后，春色依旧，故人却已各自拥有不同的人生。面对无法挽回的往事，陆游将满腔眷恋、悔恨与无奈凝结成了这首流传千古的词作。

作曲家周易将这份难以言说的痛楚转化为富有戏剧性的音乐表达。作品以带有叹息意味的旋律动机展开，声乐线条中的下行跳进与富有弹性的节奏，仿佛欲言又止的哽咽。钢琴前奏中的节奏交错与流动的音型，则在歌声出现之前便营造出不安而忧伤的气氛。最令人动容的是两组三字叠句：“错！错！错！”在一次次停顿中显露出无法排遣的悔恨；而结尾的“莫！莫！莫！”则仿佛从痛苦的追问转向无可奈何的沉默。最后一个延长的“莫”字悬留在音乐中，让那些未曾说出口的话语，最终化作一声绵长的叹息。

《钗头凤》没有为这段爱情提供圆满的结局。春天仍会到来，桃花仍会盛开，但属于两人的时光已无法重现。留下的只有一首词、一段旋律，以及那个无法回答的问题：当爱情依然存在，却再也无法相守，人究竟该如何与往事告别？

An unexpected reunion years after their separation brought a love once lost painfully back to life. Phoenix Hairpin is a lyric poem by the Southern Song dynasty poet Lu You, traditionally associated with an encounter with his former wife, Tang Wan, at Shen Garden in Shaoxing. Once deeply devoted to one another, the two had been forced apart by family opposition. Years later, spring returned as it always had, yet their lives had taken irrevocably different paths. Confronted with memories of a love that could never be restored, Lu You transformed his longing, regret, and helplessness into one of the most celebrated expressions of lost love in Chinese poetry.

Composer Zhou Yi transforms this unspoken anguish into music of striking dramatic intensity. The work unfolds through sigh-like melodic gestures, with descending leaps and flexible rhythms suggesting a voice struggling to express what words alone cannot convey. Interwoven rhythms and flowing figures in the piano introduction establish an atmosphere of unease and sorrow even before the voice enters. The emotional heart of the song lies in two repeated exclamations: “Wrong! Wrong! Wrong!” Each utterance, shaped by moments of silence, seems to carry the weight of irreparable regret. The final “No! No! No!” turns that anguish toward resignation, as though desperate questioning has given way to the recognition that nothing can be undone. The prolonged final “No” lingers in the music, allowing all that remains unspoken to dissolve into a single, extended sigh.

Phoenix Hairpin offers no reconciliation, no promise of a happier ending. Spring will return, and peach blossoms will bloom again, but the life the two lovers once shared can never be recovered. What remains is a poem, a melody, and an unanswered question: when love endures but being together is no longer possible, how does one ever truly say farewell to the past?

09

鸟儿在风中歌唱The Bird Sings in the Wind

刘聪作曲 · 樊孝斌词

Music by Liu Cong · Text by Fan Xiaobin

导赏Notes

刘聪是中国当代作曲家、沈阳音乐学院教授，艺术歌曲创作是其重要的创作领域之一；樊孝斌则是中国当代词作家。《鸟儿在风中歌唱》创作于1996年，是一首为花腔女高音而作的当代中国艺术歌曲。

作品以一只在山岗与风中盘旋的鸟儿为主人公。它的歌声并非自由欢快的鸣唱，而带着凄凉与不安：熟悉的家园已经变得危险，它不敢飞回自己的村庄，而它的伴侣也遭到人类的囚禁与伤害。鸟儿的呼唤因此逐渐超越了一个动物的故事，成为对失去家园、自由与生命安全的哀叹。作品借这一拟人化的叙事，将对自然与生命的怜惜转化为对人与自然关系的反思。

作品最具辨识度的部分是后段自由而高亢的花腔。急速流动的音型和不断攀升的旋律既让人联想到鸟儿振翅、盘旋与鸣叫，也成为它对自由最直接的呼唤。炫技在这里并非单纯展示声乐技巧，而成为作品情感的一部分：那仿佛是一只生命在风中奋力歌唱，也让这首作品最终从一个凄美的故事延伸为对自由、生命与自然的深切呼吁。

Liu Cong is a contemporary Chinese composer and professor at the Shenyang Conservatory of Music, with art song forming an important part of his creative work. Fan Xiaobin is a contemporary Chinese lyricist. Composed in 1996, The Bird Sings in the Wind is a contemporary Chinese art song written for coloratura soprano.

The song centers on a bird circling above the hills and singing into the wind. Its voice, however, is not one of carefree freedom, but of sorrow and unease. The home it once knew has become a place of danger; it no longer dares to return to its village, while its companion has suffered captivity and harm at human hands. The bird’s cry therefore grows beyond the story of a single creature, becoming a lament for the loss of home, freedom, and safety. Through this personified narrative, the song transforms compassion for nature and living beings into a broader reflection on the relationship between humanity and the natural world.

The most distinctive section comes later, in the free and soaring coloratura writing. Rapid figurations and ever-rising lines evoke the bird’s wings, circling flight, and cries, while also becoming its most direct expression of longing for freedom. Here, virtuosity is not merely a display of vocal brilliance, but an essential part of the song’s emotional language: the image is of a living creature singing with all its strength into the wind, transforming a poignant story into a powerful appeal for freedom, life, and the natural world.

诔词Requiescat

张瑞作曲 · 徐志摩译诗

Music by Zhang Rui · Chinese translation by Xu Zhimo

导赏Notes

张瑞是中国当代作曲家、天津音乐学院作曲指挥系副教授。《诔词》采用徐志摩对英国诗人Matthew Arnold 诗作的中文译文谱写，并收录于张瑞2009年出版的《雪花的快乐-张瑞艺术歌曲集》中。徐志摩的译文最早发表于 1925年，在保留原诗哀悼意味的同时，也融入了他富有节奏感与诗意的中文语言。

诗歌从“散上玫瑰花”的送别场景开始，却没有停留在死亡本身。逝者经历过生命的欢欣，也曾在“火热与扰攘”的世界中疲惫挣扎；如今，死亡反而使她获得了生前始终渴望的宁静与自由。由玫瑰、寂静、安眠到最终“攀登那死的插天的高楼”，诗中的意象一步步将现实的哀悼引向更辽阔的精神世界。死亡因此既意味着离别，也成为灵魂从尘世束缚中得到释放的一种想象。

张瑞的音乐并不以外在的戏剧性表现悲痛，而更多通过克制而内敛的音乐语言，使悲伤逐渐从平静的叙述中浮现。声乐旋律中细微而不安的音程变化，使音乐始终笼罩着一种无法完全平息的忧伤。

《诔词》并不只诉说失去。随着诗歌从“倦了” 走向“和平”，从人世的“局促” 走向灵魂的自由，悲恸也逐渐转化为一种安静而深沉的接受。最终，作品留下的并非强烈的哭泣，而是一种近乎静止的告别：逝者已经远去，而她所渴望的和平，终于属于她。

Zhang Rui is a contemporary Chinese composer and Associate Professor of Composition and Conducting at the Tianjin Conservatory of Music. Requiescat sets Xu Zhimo’s Chinese translation of a poem by the English poet Matthew Arnold and was later included in Zhang Rui’s 2009 art song collection The Joy of Snowflakes. Xu’s translation was first published in 1925. While preserving the elegiac spirit of Arnold’s original, his Chinese rendering brings to the poem his own distinctive sense of rhythm, imagery, and poetic lyricism.

The poem opens with the farewell image, “Strew on her roses, roses,” yet its meditation reaches beyond death itself. The departed woman once brought joy to the world, even as “her heart was tired, tired.” Her life had turned endlessly “in mazes of heat and sound,” but in death she finally receives the peace for which her soul had longed. From roses and stillness to sleep and, ultimately, “the vasty hall of death,” the poem gradually transforms an intimate act of mourning into a vision of spiritual release. Death becomes not only a separation, but also an imagined liberation from the confines of earthly life.

Zhang Rui does not portray grief through overt dramatic gestures. Instead, sorrow emerges gradually from a restrained and inward musical language. The subtle, unsettled intervallic shifts allow an undercurrent of grief to remain ever present.

Requiescat speaks not only of loss. As the poem moves from “tired, tired” toward “peace,” and from the spirit once “cabin’d” by earthly existence toward freedom, grief gradually gives way to a quiet and profound acceptance. What remains at the end is not an anguished cry, but an almost suspended farewell: she has passed beyond this world, and the peace for which she longed is, at last, her own.

歌Song

张瑞作曲 · 徐志摩译诗

Music by Zhang Rui · Chinese translation by Xu Zhimo

导赏Notes

张瑞是中国当代作曲家、天津音乐学院作曲指挥系副教授，艺术歌曲是其重要的创作领域。《歌》采用徐志摩对英国维多利亚时期诗人 Christina Rossetti 诗作 Song （When I am dead, my dearest）的中文译文谱写。张瑞于1997年创作了这首作品；Rossetti 的原诗发表于1862年，以极为简洁而克制的语言思考死亡、爱与记忆。

诗歌从一句近乎平静的嘱托开始：“我死了的时候，亲爱的，别为我唱悲伤的歌。”诗中的主人公并不要求爱人以玫瑰、柏树或长久的哀悼纪念自己，而只是愿让青草覆盖墓地，让雨水与露珠自然落下。甚至对于最私人、最难割舍的记忆，她也留下了自由：“假如你愿意，请记着我；要是你甘心，忘了我。”这种对记忆与遗忘同样坦然的态度，使死亡不再只是终结，而更像是一种对世间执念的缓慢放下。

《歌》最动人的地方，也许正在于它对死亡几乎没有恐惧。诗中的人最终进入一个“阳光不升起也不消翳”的悠长暮色，在那里，尘世的声音、雨水与夜莺都渐渐远去，而记忆本身也失去了必须被抓住的重量。作品没有为“遗忘”赋予残酷的意义，反而让记得与忘记都成为爱的一种可能。最终留下的不是悲恸，而是一种温柔、安静而近乎释然的告别。

Zhang Rui is a contemporary Chinese composer and Associate Professor of Composition and Conducting at the Tianjin Conservatory of Music, with art song forming an important part of her creative work. Song sets Xu Zhimo’s Chinese translation of Christina Rossetti’s Song (When I am dead, my dearest). Zhang composed the work in 1997; Rossetti’s original poem, first published in 1862, reflects on death, love, and memory with striking simplicity and restraint.

The poem begins with an almost serene request: “When I am dead, my dearest, / Sing no sad songs for me.” The speaker asks for neither roses nor cypress trees, nor for any lasting ritual of mourning. Instead, she imagines green grass growing above her grave, touched naturally by rain and dew. Even in the most intimate realm of memory, she leaves her beloved entirely free: “And if thou wilt, remember, / And if thou wilt, forget.” This equal acceptance of remembrance and forgetting transforms death from a mere ending into a gradual release from the attachments of earthly life

Perhaps the most touching moment of Song is its near absence of fear in the face of death. The speaker ultimately enters a long twilight, “That doth not rise nor set.” where the sounds of the earthly world,the rain and the nightingale, slowly recede, and even memory itself no longer needs to be held tightly. Forgetting is not presented as cruelty; rather, both remembering and forgetting become possible expressions of love. What remains is not grief, but a farewell of remarkable tenderness, stillness, and acceptance.

敕勒歌Song of the Chi-Le Plain

刘洲作曲 · 邓垚、王威编配 · 北朝民歌、薛保勤词

Music by Liu Zhou · Arranged by Deng Yao and Wang Wei · Text from a Northern Dynasties folk song, expanded by Xue Baoqin

导赏Notes

《敕勒歌》源自中国南北朝时期流传于北方的一首古老民歌，后来收入宋代郭茂倩编纂的《乐府诗集》。原诗以极为凝练的语言描绘敕勒川辽阔壮美的草原景象：“天似穹庐，笼盖四野。天苍苍，野茫茫，风吹草低见牛羊。”千百年来，这几句诗成为中国文学中最具代表性的草原意象之一，也寄托着北方游牧民族对家园与自然的深厚情感。

现代版本由薛保勤在原诗基础上扩写歌词，刘洲作曲，并由邓垚、王威编配。新增的诗句从第一人称视角展开：“心随天地走，意被牛羊牵”，将古诗中静态而辽阔的草原景象转化为一个人在天地之间行走、凝望与感受的内心旅程。大漠孤烟、落日、月亮、篝火与牛羊依次出现，使古老的敕勒川不仅成为历史中的风景，也成为一种关于故乡、自由与辽阔天地的当代想象。

当古老的“敕勒川，阴山下”最终再次响起时，作品仿佛跨越千年，让现代人的声音与古老民歌彼此回应。《敕勒歌》所描绘的不只是草原的壮丽，也是一种人与土地之间深刻而本能的归属感：天地辽阔，而人的情感最终仍被故乡牵引。

Song of the Chile originates from an ancient folk song of northern China dating from the Northern and Southern Dynasties period, and was later preserved in the Collection of Yuefu Poetry compiled by the Song-dynasty scholar Guo Maoqian. In a few remarkably vivid lines, the original poem evokes the vast beauty of the Chile grasslands: “The sky is like a great dome, embracing the wilderness on every side. The heavens stretch blue above the boundless plain; when the wind bends the grass, cattle and sheep appear.” For centuries, these verses have remained among the most iconic images of the northern grasslands in Chinese literature, expressing the deep connection between nomadic peoples, their homeland, and the natural world.

In this modern version, Xue Baoqin expands upon the ancient poem, with music by Liu Zhou and an arrangement by Deng Yao and Wang Wei. The newly added text adopts a first-person perspective: “My heart follows heaven and earth; my thoughts are drawn by the cattle and sheep.” The expansive landscape of the ancient poem becomes an inward journey of someone moving through, observing, and experiencing the world around them. Solitary smoke over the desert, the setting sun, moonlight, campfires, and grazing herds emerge one by one, transforming the ancient Chile grasslands from a distant historical landscape into a contemporary vision of homeland, freedom, and the immensity of nature.

When the ancient words “Chile River, beneath the Yin Mountains” return near the end, the music seems to reach across centuries, allowing a modern voice to answer an ancient folk song. Song of the Chile ultimately portrays more than the grandeur of the grasslands: it expresses a profound and instinctive bond between people and the land they call home. Beneath the vastness of heaven and earth, the heart is still drawn homeward.
